One of the significant hurdles in bringing the motor imagery (MI) electroencephalography (EEG) based brain-computer interface (BCI) technology out from the lab environment is the unstable classification performance due to the complex behavior of EEG with nonlinear dynamic properties. The subspace method (SM) of pattern recognition is a statistical method where each class of data is represented by a separate subspace spanned by a set of basis vectors, which are traditionally the orthogonal vectors learned by principal component analysis (PCA). Recognition of a new pattern is based on its structural similarity with the learned subspaces. In this paper, a novel framework using local and global regressive mapping (LGRM) based manifold learning is proposed to learn the subspaces while implementing the subspace method of classification on MI EEG. The proposed method is evaluated on BCI competition IV dataset 2b and compared with the linear subspace learning method using PCA and its nonlinear counterpart using kernel PCA (KPCA). It has been reported in terms of classification accuracy that the proposed method outperformed both the PCA and KPCA-based subspace methods and is comparable with many state-of-the-art methods.
